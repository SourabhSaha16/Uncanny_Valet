Uncanny Valet: Chaos Redesign
Nov 9, 2026 · @Sourabh Saha
Proposal: drop the mafia, debt and strikes. Rebuild Uncanny Valet as a level-based chaos game in the Overcooked mould: short frantic shifts, three-star scores, simple verbs layered into juggling, and failures that are funny rather than punishing. This sits beside the main design doc until the lead folds it in.
1. Why these games hook players
Overcooked, Catastronauts, Moving Out and Plate Up all run on the same engine: too many small, easy tasks at once, in a space that fights you, scored against a clear target, with instant retry. No single task is hard. Juggling them is.

Why solo works: Overcooked gives a solo player two chefs and a swap button. The game becomes a delegation puzzle: park one chef on a slow task, run the other. Solo play is less about reflexes and more about planning a route through parallel timers. Uncanny Valet should be designed for that from day one (section 6).
2. New theme: a doomed family valet firm at the world's weirdest venues
You work for Uncanny Valet Co., a tiny family valet firm that takes the jobs nobody else will: haunted mansions, monster-truck rallies, a wedding on a cliff edge, a robot convention. The title now means the venues and the cars are uncanny, not the valet.
• Boss: Mrs. Okafor (kept from the old cards), the cheerful founder who believes every car can be saved. She replaces the mafia boss and fills the Onion King role: briefings, star verdicts, terrible pep talks.
• Rival: ParkCorp, a sleek robot-valet company bidding for every venue contract. Beat their target score on a venue's final lot to win the contract and unlock the next venue on the map.
• Stakes: light and silly. If ParkCorp wins the city, Okafor has to sell the company and the family car collection. No death, no strikes, no debt.
• The pun lines stay. The valet is relentlessly upbeat. When a car comes back damaged, his goodbye line spins it: a car with no doors gets "Great airflow on this one!", a scorched EV gets "Fully charged, and then some!". This keeps the original's best joke without the crime.
What is cut: mafia jobs, sabotage, the strike system, debt and the hidden chase ending. Police raids return as a venue hazard (section 5). The final chase survives as a ParkCorp showdown level.
3. Core loop, verbs, tickets, scoring
A shift is a 4-minute level on one lot. Patrons arrive, you park their cars, service whatever their ticket asks for, and hand each car back when they return. Tips are the score.
1. A car pulls up at the curb with a ticket floating above it: car icon, colour, requests, patience bar.
2. Grab the keys and drive it to a free spot (or straight to a station).
3. Its keys go on the key board by the booth. The board shows which spot each car is in, colour-matched.
4. While it sits, service it: wash, fix, charge, refuel, refit whatever the ticket asks.
5. The patron reappears at the curb. A pickup bell rings and their patience starts draining.
6. Find the car, drive it to the curb, hop out. Tip, combo and pun line.

Scoring
Tip = base by car class (hatchback $10, sports $20, limo $35) + requests met + speed bonus from patience left, minus damage, clamped at $0. Then multiplied by the combo.
• Combo: each back-to-back happy patron raises the multiplier one step, from 1x to a cap of 4x. A missed request, a wrecked car or an angry patron resets it.
• Angry patron: if patience runs out, they storm off to the lot to find their own car (they drive badly, crash on the way out). You lose the tip and take a $15 complaint penalty from the shift score. This is the only way the score goes down.
• Stars: each lot has 1, 2 and 3-star tip thresholds. Stars unlock the next lots. A hidden 4-star target appears once a lot is 3-starred.
• Bonus objectives per lot, optional: no fires, perfect parks only, no car above 25% damage.
4. Chaos systems
The rule for every system here: easy to trigger, loud to notice, quick to fix, funny to watch. Chaos should cost seconds and combo, never a whole level.
Driving: arcade, forgiving, slightly floaty
• Cars are dynamic bodies with five stats: top speed, acceleration, turn rate, grip, braking. Plus mass, which decides who wins a shove.
• Grip is the comedy dial. High grip feels like a go-kart; low grip slides like a shopping trolley on ice.
• Steering scales with speed, so a parked car can't spin on the spot. Handbrake drops grip for a drift.
• Parking is forgiving. A spot accepts a car that is mostly inside and nearly stopped, then nudges it straight. A clean, aligned stop scores a Perfect Park bonus (+$2, a chime and a sparkle). Skill is rewarded; sloppiness is never blocked.
• Parked cars can be shoved, but with heavy damping. Bumping a row of cars is a comic domino, not a catastrophe.

EV overcharging and fire
This is the "forgot the pot on the stove" moment, and the main source of panic in later venues.
1. Plug an EV in. The charge meter fills over about 20 seconds.
2. At 100% the car chimes and the meter turns amber. You have about 8 seconds to unplug it.
3. Past that, sparks and smoke for 5 seconds, then the car catches fire.
4. Fire spreads to the neighbouring spot every 6 seconds and spreads into puddles of spilt fuel instantly.
5. A burning car takes damage until it is wrecked. A car fully burnt pays nothing and resets the combo.
6. Grab an extinguisher (two on every EV lot) and spray. Sand buckets work too and are funnier.
The fire spreading between cars is what turns one mistake into a snowball, like Overcooked's kitchen fires. Smart players park EVs away from other cars; greedy players pack them in.
Other fire sources: an overheating engine, a car dragging a bumper over a fuel spill, a fireworks crate at the wedding venue.

6. Single-player design
Solo is the primary mode (it is a web game). Co-op for 2–4 players on one keyboard or gamepads is a later module, and every level must work for both.
SUPERSEDED 2026-11-11: Kev, the task board and the swap button were dropped; the game has one valet. The text below is kept for history only.
Decision: two valets, swap button, and the one you're not controlling is Kev, a rookie AI. Overcooked's idle chef just stands there. Kev doesn't: left alone for 2 seconds, he picks a task off the task board, more or less at random, and does it. He is useful and unreliable in roughly equal parts, which is both the help and the joke.
• Rudimentary on purpose. Kev chooses by weighted random, not by what matters most. He might wash a car nobody asked to wash while a patron fumes at the curb.
• He drives like a rookie: 70% top speed, wide turns, and he suffers every fault the car has. Kev in a reverse-only car is a set piece.
• The player always wins. Swapping to Kev, or grabbing the car or item he is using, cancels his task cleanly and returns it to the board.
• Optional later: point and nudge. Aim at a car or station and press Horn to make it Kev's next pick. Not in P2.
Task design (modular)
Everything Kev can do is a task on a shared task board. Rule systems post tasks in reaction to events; Kev's brain only picks from the board. Adding a new job for Kev means adding one task class and one generator, nothing else.

Kev pathfinds over the lot's lane graph (from ILotLayout) and steers toward the next waypoint. Weights and timings live in a KevConfig ScriptableObject so designers can make him dimmer or sharper without code.
Design for parallel timers. Every level needs at least two things that run on their own while you are elsewhere: a charge meter, a wash cycle, a patron's stay, a snowplough sweep. That is where planning lives, and planning is what makes a solo player feel clever.
Other solo hooks:
• Instant retry from the pause menu and results screen, with the lot already loaded.
• Personal best ghost: your best run's score ticks along at the top of the screen, like ParkCorp's.
• Daily lot: one seeded level per day, same for everyone, with a web leaderboard. Cheap to build, strong reason to come back.
• Results screen stats that are worth screenshotting: hubcaps lost, cars set on fire, metres driven in reverse, ducklings honked at.
• Assist options: slower patience drain, no fire spread, parking autopilot. Off by default, never locked behind failure.
7. Progression, comedy and juice
Progression is a world map of venues gated by stars, like Overcooked's overworld. Stars also unlock cosmetics only: valet outfits, horn sounds, key-board trinkets, a hubcap collection. No stat upgrades, so every level stays balanced around one skill curve.
Comedy comes from systems, not scripts. Write rules that collide and let the jokes happen:
• Patrons react to everything with a speech-bubble icon: a heart for a perfect park, a skull when their car is on fire, a tiny tear for a missing door.
• Pedestrians, pigeons and a family of ducklings cross the lot. Hitting nothing is the challenge; honking them away is the joke.
• The pun goodbye line plays on every return, picked by the car's worst state: on fire, missing parts, wrong colour after a paint spill, dog still inside.
• Patrons who storm off drive their own car out terribly, ploughing through your neat rows.
• Rare surprises: a limo with 14 passengers climbing out, a car full of bees, a patron who returns for the wrong car and drives it away.
Juice checklist (cheap, and most of the feel):
• Screen shake scaled to impact force; a short freeze-frame on big crashes.
• Squash and stretch on cars when they brake hard or land off a ramp.
• Tyre marks that stay on the lot all shift: the lot becomes a record of your chaos.
• Coins pop out of a happy patron and fly to the tip counter; the combo number grows and wobbles.
• Each problem has its own sound: pickup bell, overcharge beep rising in pitch, fire whoosh, alarm.
• A countdown, an "Order up!" style shift start, and a big stamp-style star reveal at the end.
8. Unity implementation and impact on the current plan
The existing architecture survives almost untouched. The objective model (conditions checked against a car, plus an outcome) is exactly an Overcooked order. What changes is the run-state layer.
Unity notes for top-down pixel-art driving
• Car controller: Rigidbody2D with gravity 0. Each FixedUpdate: add forward force from throttle, then remove sideways velocity by the grip factor (multiply the right-vector component by 1 − grip). Rotate by turn rate × steer × clamp(speed / 3, 0, 1). Handbrake lowers grip to about 0.2.
• Faults as input and stat decorators: an IDriveModifier interface that edits the input vector or stats before the controller reads them. Swapped steering, stuck accelerator and reverse-only are one small class each, matching the doc's "new rules are new classes" principle.
• Rotating pixel art: freely rotating pixel sprites shimmer. Use sprite stacking (a car drawn as 8–16 horizontal slices, drawn offset by one pixel each) for smooth rotation that still reads as pixel art, or pre-render 16 or 32 facing angles. Use the Pixel Perfect Camera with upscaled render texture so everything snaps to the same grid.
• Damage: the collision impulse from OnCollisionEnter2D (contact.normalImpulse) above a threshold adds damage. Damage stages swap sprite slices and spawn detached parts as separate Rigidbody2D debris with their own lifetimes.
• Fire: a simple per-spot heat value, not particles-as-logic. Fire raises neighbours' heat each tick; at a threshold they ignite. The extinguisher is a cone overlap that lowers heat. Particles are cosmetic only.
• WebGL budget: cap debris at about 40 pieces and pool them; keep physics bodies under about 60 per lot.

9. Instructions for Claude Code agents
This section is written for Claude Code agents pivoting the existing Unity project on a remote machine. Agents cannot read this Claude Doc: the lead exports it as Markdown and commits it to docs/design/chaos_redesign.md before any agent starts. Read that file and docs/design/uncanny_valet_design.md in full first.
Ground rules
• This redesign overrides the old design doc wherever they conflict. Mafia jobs, sabotage, debt, strikes, the phone UI and the hidden chase are cut.
• Never edit design docs. Log every change, decision and blocker in docs/changelogs/<your-agent-name>.md, newest first.
• Keep the architecture: four layers, a typed event bus, Services as the only registry, one owner per piece of state, rules as pure classes behind interfaces.
• No magic numbers. Tuning lives in ScriptableObjects: ShiftConfig, CarDefinition, PedestrianConfig, AudioConfig, later KevConfig.
• Web rules hold: no threads, no System.IO, no blocking calls, build compression disabled for GitHub Pages.
• Build scenes and prefabs from editor scripts (menu items or -executeMethod batch methods) rather than hand-writing large .unity or .prefab YAML. Small YAML edits are fine.
• Stop and ask the lead when an open question blocks you or the doc is ambiguous. Write the question in your changelog and the PR description; do not invent major design.
Pivot steps, in order
1. Branch pivot/chaos from main. Run all tests and record the baseline in your changelog.
2. Remove the mafia layer. Delete DebtLedger, StrikeTracker, IDebtView, mafia objective policies and the phone UI. Replace NightClock with ShiftClock (seconds remaining, start, last-30-seconds and end events) behind IGameClock.

4. Import art. The lead downloads the three MetroCity .rar files and commits them to art-src/metrocity/ at the repo root. Extract them with 7-Zip (install p7zip if missing) into Assets/_Project/Art/ThirdParty/MetroCity/{Environment,Cars,Characters}. Keep originals untouched and add CREDITS.md (CC0, by JIK-A-4).
5. Pixel settings. Inspect the sprites to find the pack's native tile size and use that as one project-wide pixels-per-unit. Add an AssetPostprocessor that sets Point filtering, no compression and that PPU on everything under Art/. Use the Pixel Perfect Camera with a low reference resolution (start at 480×270) and upscale render texture on.
6. Build Grandma's Diner from an editor script: Tilemap layers for ground, road markings, sidewalk, buildings and colliders, plus ILotLayout data (spots, curb zone, lane graph, crosswalks, pedestrian paths).
7. Car controller, IDriveModifier stack and impact damage (section 8 notes).
8. Pedestrians and honk; patrons, tickets, patience and key board.
9. Audio: an AudioMixer with Music and SFX groups, a stem-based MusicDirector driven by combo and clock events, pooled SFX sources.
10. HUD, results screen, retry, title screen. WebGL build and GitHub Pages deploy.
